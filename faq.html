Frequently asked questions about the northern lights

Everything you need to know to chase the aurora — from Kp values to what to wear at -25 °C.

About RepoTracker / Aurora Tracker

The name RepoTracker comes from a Finnish belief that the northern lights are created when a fire fox, "Repo", strikes the snow with its tail, sending sparks into the sky.

Who is behind this project? Aurora Tracker is a solo project, designed and maintained by a single Northern Lights enthusiast. From coding the maps to tracking solar data — everything you see here is the result of one person's passion for the aurora.

Because this is a one-person project, your feedback and support mean the world. Every coffee donated helps cover server costs and API fees that keep the real-time data running for everyone.

When is the best time to see the Northern Lights?

The Northern Lights are best seen on dark, clear nights in autumn and spring. The best time is usually between 9 p.m. and 2 a.m. In northern Finland a Kp index of 3–4 is enough; in the south you usually need Kp 5–6 or higher.

What do the values on the map mean?

The Kp index describes geomagnetic activity. The higher the value, the more likely auroras are visible:

  • Kp 0–2: very low probability
  • Kp 3–4: visible in northern Finland
  • Kp 5+: possible in southern Finland
How does cloud cover affect visibility?

Clouds block the auroras completely. Always check the cloud cover before heading out — a clear sky is essential.

Do I need a dark place?

Yes. Light pollution in cities reduces visibility significantly. Move away from street lights and choose an open area with a clear view of the northern horizon.

It is still possible to see auroras in towns like Rovaniemi — just step into a slightly darker spot and you have a good chance when the forecast is favorable.

Tip: sometimes a phone camera picks up the aurora before your eyes do.

Can the Northern Lights be seen in southern Finland?

Yes, but less frequently. A high Kp index (≥ 5–6) and clear skies are required.

How do I know when the Northern Lights will appear?

Use real-time tools like Aurora Tracker for live forecasts. You can also follow data from NOAA SWPC and the Finnish Meteorological Institute.

What are solar wind and IMF Bz?

The solar wind is a stream of charged particles from the Sun that delivers energy to Earth's magnetic field. The IMF Bz value indicates the orientation of the interplanetary magnetic field — a strong negative Bz greatly increases the chance of auroras.

What should you keep in mind when going hunting for the Northern Lights?

Dress warmly — one thick coat is not the best option. Several thin layers under a proper winter coat keep you warmer and give flexibility.

Wear warm shoes and gloves, and bring extra clothes if you plan to be outside for a long time.

If you are travelling by rental car, stay on plowed public roads. Do not drive on poorly maintained or unplowed forest roads — you can get stuck in the snow and help may take a long time to arrive in winter.

If you head out on your own, respect private yards, military areas and any place where you are unsure whether entry is allowed.

Tip: be careful where you stop the car. Bus stops are ideal. Avoid stopping on the road to prevent dangerous situations.
